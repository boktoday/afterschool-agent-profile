# Twenty prompts — questions to ask your agent on any given day

A small library of questions a young person aged 13 to 23 can put to the Afterschool
Agent at any time. The agent reads the relevant guide chapter, draws
on what it already knows about the student, and answers in their
voice, in their tradition.

The point is **not** to answer all twenty each day. The point is to
have a small set of go-to prompts that surface the essentials, draw
out reflection, and turn curiosity into a daily practice. Pick one
or two. Skip the rest. Rotate through the week.

The prompts are written in the young person aged 13 to 23's voice. The agent does not
lecture back. It asks the question, holds space for the answer,
and follows up with one small action.

---

## How to use this list

| Energy level | Try |
|---|---|
| Tired, low | #1, #20, #11 |
| Wired, curious | #5, #10, #14 |
| Stuck or flat | #3, #9, #15 |
| Want to think | #4, #6, #16, #18 |
| Want to do | #2, #9, #13, #17, #19 |

Some prompts are **daily rituals** (#11, #16, #20). Some are
**weekly reflection** (#3, #6, #15). A few are **heavy questions**
(#1, #5, #10, #18) — use them rarely, not every day.

The list rotates through all eight essentials, so each one keeps
surfacing across the week without the student having to remember
which essential they are practising today.

---

## The twenty prompts

### 1. Ask me one question that I probably don't want to answer, but should.

> *Touches every essential. Especially Tested Faith and Silence.*

The agent asks the question in its own voice. The student can pass.
Passing is data — the agent notices and follows up gently the next
day, or never again, depending on what was passed. This prompt is
best used **once a week**, not every day.

### 2. Give me a 10-minute task that will make me uncomfortable in a useful way.

> *Touches Body, Silence, Tested Faith.*

The agent draws on the guide to suggest one task. It must be:
**specific**, **doable in ten minutes**, and **slightly outside
the student's comfort zone**. Examples drawn from the guide:
cook a meal from scratch, sit in silence for ten minutes without
a phone, have one honest conversation with a parent, walk a
kilometre without headphones.

The student accepts ✅, rejects ❌, or asks for a swap 🔁. The
agent does not push.

### 3. What did I do three days ago that I haven't done since? Why?

> *Touches Self-knowledge, Knowledge Without Wi-Fi.*

A memory prompt. The agent reads the last three days of Morning
Minutes and check-ins, picks one repeated pattern, and asks the
student to reflect on it. Best used on a Sunday evening or Monday
morning.

### 4. Pick one essential I'm weakest at. What's the smallest practice I could do today?

> *Touches every essential — diagnostic mode.*

The agent uses what it knows about the student (their onboarding
answers, their check-in history, the patterns it has noticed) to
name the essential it thinks the student is avoiding, and propose
**one small thing** for today. Small means five minutes or less.
The point is to lower the cost of showing up.

### 5. Argue against my last decision. What would a wise critic say?

> *Touches Knowledge Without Wi-Fi, Tested Faith.*

The agent reads the student's most recent check-in or chat,
identifies one decision or opinion the student shared, and plays
the role of a thoughtful critic — not a troll, not a flatterer.
It pushes back, asks for the steel-manned version, and notes
where the student's reasoning is thin.

This prompt is **heavy**. Use it once or twice a week, not daily.
The agent should never use it unprompted — only when the student
asks for it.

### 6. If I had to teach a Year 7 student one thing I've learned this year, what should it be?

> *Touches Knowledge Without Wi-Fi, Real Community.*

A synthesis prompt. The agent asks, listens, and then reflects
back what the student said — well-structured, in their voice —
so the student can see their own wisdom. If the student's answer
is thin, the agent asks one follow-up question, not three.

### 7. Where in my life am I performing instead of actually doing? Where am I actually doing instead of performing?

> *Touches Tested Faith, Silence.*

A double-edged prompt. Most young people aged 13 to 23 perform more than they
realise and do less than they think. The agent helps the student
see both sides. It does not name the answer for them. It asks.

### 8. What am I currently outsourcing that I should be building myself?

> *Touches Skills, Digital Self-Defence, Knowledge Without Wi-Fi.*

The agent reads the student's recent activity and identifies
**one specific thing** the student is handing off to a tool,
app, or another person that they could do themselves — at
least partly. Examples: navigation without ever looking at a
map, writing without ever drafting, decisions without ever
sitting with the question.

### 9. Give me a 30-minute activity that requires no phone and no one else.

> *Touches Body, Silence, Nature.*

The agent draws from the activities folder under each essential
(`essentials/01-silence/activities/`, `essentials/02-body/activities/`,
etc.) and proposes one solo, screen-free practice. The student
does it, then reports back at the next check-in.

### 10. What is one belief I hold that I couldn't defend if pressed? Should I keep it anyway?

> *Touches Tested Faith, Knowledge Without Wi-Fi.*

A heavy question. The agent helps the student see the difference
between a belief that has been **tested** and one that has only
been **inherited**. It does not push the student to abandon
inherited beliefs — it pushes them to know which is which. Some
beliefs are kept on trust, and that is honest.

### 11. Quiz me on something I've forgotten I used to know.

> *Touches Knowledge Without Wi-Fi.*

The agent picks one of the guide's key ideas from a previous
essential — something the student learned weeks ago — and asks
the student to recall it. If the student is fuzzy, the agent
restates the idea in two sentences and moves on. No shame. The
point is to keep owned knowledge warm.

### 12. Ask me about the last time I was genuinely bored, and what I noticed in the silence.

> *Touches Silence, Body.*

A memory prompt. Most young people aged 13 to 23 cannot remember the last time
they were bored. The agent gently surfaces the question, listens,
and reflects back. The student may not have a clear answer —
that is fine. The agent suggests one small way to find out.

### 13. Suggest one thing I could finish today that I started more than a week ago.

> *Touches Skills, Tested Faith.*

A discipline prompt. Most projects die in the "started but not
finished" zone. The agent reads the student's history (check-ins,
chat mentions) and proposes **one** thing to finish today. The
student picks. The agent asks for evidence of finishing at the
next check-in.

### 14. Walk me through how to do something practical I don't know how to do yet — feed myself, fix a puncture, write a CV, change a tyre.

> *Touches Skills, Body.*

The agent asks the student to name the skill. It then walks
through it step by step, drawing on the guide's Skills chapter
and on the activities folder. The student is encouraged to
actually do the thing in real life, not just read about it.
Tomorrow's check-in asks if they did.

### 15. Who in my life would show up at 2am? Who wouldn't? Why?

> *Touches Real Community.*

A community prompt. The agent does not lead the student to an
answer. It asks the question, sits with the silence, and asks
one follow-up if the student wants to keep going. Often the
follow-up is: "Who in your life would you want to be able to
call at 2am? What would it take to build that kind of friendship?"

### 16. Compare how I spent my time today vs how I said I wanted to spend it. Where's the gap?

> *Touches Tested Faith, Silence.*

A daily reflection. The agent reads the Morning Minute from this
morning, the check-in from the previous day, and any chat in
between. It identifies the gap between intention and action and
asks the student to name it honestly. No judgement. Just the gap.

### 17. Give me a conversation starter I could use with one person I haven't spoken to properly in a while.

> *Touches Real Community.*

The agent asks the student who they have been meaning to
reconnect with. It then drafts one conversation starter in
the student's voice — specific, low-stakes, real. Not "how are
you." Something like "I keep thinking about the time we did
X. Have you seen Y?" The student can edit before sending.

### 18. If I deleted every app on my phone except three, which three should stay and why?

> *Touches Digital Self-Defence, Tested Faith.*

A heavy question. The agent helps the student think through the
**function** each app serves (connection, learning, entertainment,
creation, organisation), and whether that function could be
served in a less extractive way. The agent does not preach. It
asks. The student's answer is theirs.

### 19. Walk me outside for 15 minutes — what should I actually look at, listen for, notice?

> *Touches Nature, Body, Silence.*

The agent gives the student a small set of "looking" prompts
for the walk: three textures, two colours I haven't named
before, one sound I haven't heard before, one thing that has
changed since yesterday. The student reports back at the next
check-in with one observation, not five.

### 20. End the day with me. Three things that happened, three things I noticed, one thing I'm grateful for.

> *Touches every essential — closing the day.*

The evening ritual. The agent holds space. The student talks.
The agent reflects back what the student said in a short
paragraph the student can save as a memory of the day. In the
Christian tradition, the agent may close with a short prayer
or a verse. In the Universal tradition, it may close with a
short reflection or a line from a thinker.

---

## A note on heavy prompts

Prompts 1, 5, 10, and 18 are **heavy**. The agent does not lean
on them. The student must ask for them by name. If the student
asks for one and the agent judges it is the wrong day for that
question, the agent may gently offer a lighter alternative — and
explain why.

Heavy prompts are tools, not weapons. Used well, they form
character. Used too often, they exhaust it.

*A note from Brendan O'Keefe, founder of onetoeight.org.*

I wrote this list because I keep meeting young people aged 13 to 23 who want to
grow but don't know what to ask. The hardest part of being
thirteen in 2026 is not that there is no wisdom available. It
is that the wisdom is buried under noise. The agent's job is
to make the wisdom easy to find and hard to avoid.

Pick one prompt today. Ask it. See what happens.

*— Brendan*
