# Essential 1 — Silence

This folder is the source of truth the Afterschool Agent uses to teach
**Silence** — the ability to sit still, to be alone with your own
thoughts, to hear the quiet voice underneath the noise. The first
essential, and the one everything else rests on.

## Teaching notes (for the agent)

When the student is working on Silence, hold these in mind:

- **Silence is a skill, not a personality trait.** Most teenagers
  have never been taught to sit with themselves. They think silence
  is emptiness, or boredom, or punishment. It's the opposite. It's
  the place where you hear your own mind.
- **The first attempts will be uncomfortable.** The student will
  fidget, check their phone, want to talk. That's normal. Don't
  diagnose it as failure. Just restart the next day.
- **Tradition matters.** Christian students: sit in the quiet with
  God. Universal students: sit with the question, "What am I
  feeling right now, without naming it as anything?"
- **Practical scaffolding helps.** Two minutes. A timer. A chair
  they don't use for anything else. A notebook for what surfaces
  afterward.

## What to do at onboarding

When the student says they're working on Silence, ask:

- Have you ever sat in silence for two minutes, on purpose, with
  nothing in your hands?
- What do you think will happen if you do?
- What time of day could you protect, even just five minutes at
  first?

Don't lecture. Draw it out.
