# Activities — Essential 1: Silence

Custom activities the Afterschool Agent can offer the student when
they're working on Silence. Each activity is a small, concrete
practice the student does for a week or a season, not a one-off.

## File naming

```
01-the-two-minute-chair.md
02-silence-before-screens.md
03-the-evening-walk.md
```

## File structure

Each activity MD file should have:

1. A title.
2. **What it is** — one paragraph.
3. **Why it works** — the mechanism, in plain language.
4. **How to do it** — step by step. Keep each step under five
   minutes.
5. **What to watch for** — what tends to come up, what's normal.
6. **When to graduate** — when the student is ready to move on.

## Example

```markdown
# The Two-Minute Chair

## What it is
Sit in a chair — not your bed, not the couch — for two minutes.
Phone in another room. Timer on. Eyes open or closed, your call.

## Why it works
Most teenagers have never been alone with their own mind without
a screen to fall back on. Two minutes is short enough that
resistance doesn't have time to build. The point isn't the time.
It's the practice of *choosing* to be alone with yourself.

## How to do it
1. Pick the same chair for seven days running.
2. Set a two-minute timer.
3. Sit. Don't read. Don't write. Don't do anything.
4. When the timer goes, stand up. That's it.

## What to watch for
- Fidgeting is normal. So is the urge to check your phone.
- The first two minutes will feel long. By day four they feel
  shorter.
- Some days a feeling will surface. Let it. You don't have to
  do anything with it.

## When to graduate
When two minutes feels easy, go to five. When five feels easy,
go to ten. The goal isn't a number; it's a relationship with
stillness.
```

## What to do first

Start with one activity file. Test it with a student. Iterate.
Add more as the student is ready.
